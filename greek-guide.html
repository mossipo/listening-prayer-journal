How to use the Greek Study Desk

Read the New Testament in Greek, see how each sentence is built, and grow until you need the tools less. Five minutes to read; keep it handy.

  1. Opening a passage
  2. Reading and tapping words
  3. Seeing the structure
  4. Learning the Greek
  5. A weekly rhythm
  6. Good to know

1. Opening a passage

  • From Akouō: the Greek link beside any New Testament passage, Study in Greek on a word card, or the Greek link on the Bible mark-up page.
  • On the Greek page: tap the passage name at the top to choose a book, chapter or verses. The arrows move a chapter at a time.
  • All 27 books are here. An Old Testament reference tells you the desk covers the New Testament.

2. Reading and tapping words

Read shows the Greek with a small English gloss under each word and the BSB under each verse. Use Glosses and English: Under, Beside, Off to show as much or as little help as you want.

Tap any word and a panel opens:

  • Word: its dictionary form, meaning, and the BSB words that translate it here.
  • Mark as known, Add to my deck, All its forms: your Learn buttons (see section 4).
  • Parsing in plain English, with a short note on what that tense, mood or case usually does.
  • In this sentence: the word's job (subject, object, and so on) and a link to the sentence's structure.
  • In the New Testament: how often the word occurs and where; Lexicon: the Abbott-Smith and Thayer entries; My notes on this word: your own notes, saved to your account.

While a word is open, its other forms in the passage get a dotted underline, so you can follow the word through the text.

3. Seeing the structure

Switch View to Structure. Each sentence is laid out clause by clause in Greek word order:

  • Main clauses sit at the margin; clauses that depend on them are indented under the clause they belong to, with a label such as "Adverbial clause · participle · tells more about ἔχομεν".
  • Each part has a role label: Subject, Verb, Object, Predicate, Adverbial. Tap a label to light up that whole group, with its head word marked.
  • Who does what under each clause spells out subject, verb and object. Where the subject is only in the verb, it says so ("in the verb: we").
  • A part split by other words shows as "Subject …" and "… Subject"; tapping either piece lights up all of it.
  • Flow of thought at the top lists the sentences with the usual sense of each connective (γάρ gives a reason, οὖν draws a conclusion).
  • Tree view under each sentence shows the full analysis as nested groups.

Remember: the structure is MACULA's analysis, one careful scholarly reading. Where commentators read a phrase differently (where εἰς ἄφεσιν attaches in Acts 2:38, for example), weigh them. The page never invents grammar; where the data has nothing, it says so.

4. Learning the Greek

Graded reading

Turn on Graded. Glosses disappear; words you know never show one. Tap an unknown word once to see its gloss, again for the full panel. The top of the page tells you what share of the passage you know.

The Learn sheet

Tap Learn. Everything in it is built from the passage you have open.

  • Review: your vocabulary deck. Add the passage's words (most common first) or the core words, the 309 words used 50 times or more, which make up about four in five words of the New Testament. Each word is practised Greek to English and English to Greek, with an example verse. Be honest with Missed it and Got it; the deck spaces the reviews out for you.
  • Parsing: ten words from the passage. Choose the tense, voice, mood, person and number, or the case, number and gender, then check. Mistakes are explained.
  • Paradigms: every form a word takes in the New Testament, with yours highlighted, plus model tables (the article, the noun patterns, εἰμί, λύω, δίδωμι and more). A dash means that form never occurs in the New Testament.
  • Lessons: short lessons on what the passage contains (participles, the aorist, the genitive), each with the passage's own examples to tap.
  • Progress: words known, cards due, passages studied, parsing accuracy.

A word counts as known when you mark it, or when you have recalled it both ways for at least a week of reviews. Seeing it once never counts.

5. A weekly rhythm

A suggestion for learning the Greek of your preaching text, alongside Logos rather than instead of it:

  1. Early in the week: open the text with Graded on. Read it through in Greek, tapping only what you must. Add the words you missed to your deck.
  2. Every day, five minutes: do your Review cards. This is where the vocabulary sticks.
  3. Twice in the week: one round of Parsing on the text, and read the lesson it offers.
  4. Before you go to Logos: switch to Structure and trace the main sentence yourself. Find the main verb, then what hangs on it. Write what you see in the word notes.
  5. Then: open Logos and the commentaries and compare. Note where your reading and theirs differ, and why.

Over months the share of each passage you know will climb, and the tools become a check on your reading rather than the reading itself.

6. Good to know

  • Offline: passages you have opened, and the Learn data, stay on your iPad. Changes save when you reconnect.
  • Verse numbers: where the BSB counts a few words in a different verse, the page tells you (Acts 13:38-39, for example).
  • Romans 16:25-27 is not in the SBL Greek New Testament; it comes from the Nestle 1904 text and is labelled.
  • "Matched by position": where a word's Strong's number occurs more than once in a verse, the BSB words shown for it are matched by position and marked so.
  • Sources: Greek text SBLGNT; parsing and sentence analysis MACULA Greek (Clear Bible); grammar wording and transliteration STEPBible (Tyndale House); lexicons Abbott-Smith and Thayer; English the Berean Standard Bible. Full credits are at the foot of every Greek page.
  • Your data: notes, known words and your deck are saved to your Akouō account and deleted with it.
